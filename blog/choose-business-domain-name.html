How to Choose a Domain Name for Your Business

October 28, 2026·7 min read

A domain name is the address people type, say aloud and read on a business card. It also ends up in your email addresses, your Google Business Profile and every link you ever share. Changing it later is possible, but it means redirects, new email addresses and a period of lost momentum, so it is worth spending an hour on the decision before you register anything.

1. Keep it short and easy to say

The best test is the phone test: say the address to a friend and ask them to write it down. If you have to spell it, explain a hyphen or say "that's two Ls", the name is too complicated. Aim for something people can type correctly on the first try, ideally under about fifteen characters, with no unusual spelling tricks.

2. Use your business name first

If your company is called Kask Remont, then kaskremont is the obvious choice, because people who already know you will guess it. A name made of generic keywords, such as best-cheap-renovation-tallinn, looks spammy, is hard to remember and does not help your search rankings the way it once might have. Your brand is what customers will search for and recommend; build the domain around it.

3. Pick the right ending

If you serve customers in Estonia, a .ee domain signals a local business and is what many people expect to see. A .com suits companies that plan to work internationally or already have an English-speaking audience. Newer endings such as .io or .shop can work, but unfamiliar ones are more often mistyped and can look less established. If the .ee version is free, it is often worth registering the .com too, and the other way round, so a competitor or a lookalike cannot take it.

A quick test before you buy: search the exact name on Google and in social networks. If another business with a similar name already ranks or has an active page, customers may end up on their site instead of yours.

4. Avoid hyphens, numbers and look-alike letters

Hyphens are easy to forget when someone hears the address, and numbers raise the question "digit or word?". Letter combinations that look alike, such as rn and m, or the letter l and the number 1, cause mistakes in print and email. Every extra thing a person must remember is a chance to lose them, so keep the name plain.

5. Check the name is legally and practically clear

Before you commit, make sure the name does not infringe someone else's registered trademark or an existing company name in your field. Check that the exact name is free as a social media handle too, because consistent naming makes you easier to find. Also read the name in a few languages if you serve Estonian, Russian and English speakers: a word that is harmless in one language can be awkward in another. If you are planning a multilingual site, our article on multilingual websites in Estonia explains how to structure the addresses.

6. Register it in your own name, and keep control

This is the mistake we see most often: a freelancer or an agency registers the domain under their own account, and years later the business cannot move or renew it. Make sure the registrant is your company, the account is yours and the renewal reminders go to an email address you read. Turn on auto-renewal, because an expired domain can stop your website and your email at the same time, and it may be taken by someone else.

7. Plan for email and for the future

A domain also gives you professional email, such as info@yourcompany.ee, which looks far more credible than a free webmail address. Think about whether the name will still fit if you add services or open in another city. A name tied to a single product or street can become a limitation. Finally, set up the basics around it: HTTPS, a redirect from the www and non-www versions to one main address, and a working email. These details are part of how visitors decide whether to trust you.

A short domain checklist

  • Short, easy to say and spell, based on the business name
  • The right ending for your market (.ee, .com or both)
  • No hyphens, digits or confusing letter pairs
  • Checked against trademarks, competitors and social handles
  • Registered to your company, with auto-renewal switched on
  • Email on the domain, HTTPS and one main version of the address

A good domain will not bring customers on its own, but a confusing one makes everything else harder: ads, referrals, printed materials and word of mouth. It is a small decision with a long tail, so it pays to get it right once.
