How to Rank in Google's Local Pack

September 28, 2026·8 min read

Search "plumber near me" or "best bakery in [your city]" and the first thing that shows up isn't a list of ten blue links — it's a map with three businesses underneath it, each with a star rating, a phone number and a "Directions" button. That's the local pack, and for most local searches it gets more clicks than every organic result below it combined. If your business isn't one of the three, you're invisible to a huge share of people who were ready to call right now, no matter how good your website is.

1. Why the local pack matters more than your website's rank

A regular Google ranking rewards the best page. The local pack rewards the best business for a specific searcher in a specific place, and it sits above the organic results on both desktop and mobile. Most people never scroll past it — they tap one of the three listings, check the reviews, and call or get directions. Your website still matters for closing the sale and for searches with no local intent, but for "near me" and "in [city]" searches, the local pack is the actual competition, and it runs on a different set of rules than normal SEO.

2. The three factors Google actually weighs

Google has said directly that local ranking comes down to three things. Relevance — how well your Google Business Profile matches what the person searched for, which is why picking the right categories and keeping your business description specific matters more than it seems. Distance — how far your listed address is from the searcher or the location they typed, something you can't change but should be honest about (using a fake or shared address to appear in multiple areas gets listings suspended). Prominence — how well-known and well-reviewed your business is, both on Google and across the web generally. Most businesses lose on prominence, not relevance or distance, which is the part they can most directly improve.

3. Citations and NAP consistency: the unglamorous foundation

A "citation" is any mention of your business Name, Address and Phone number (NAP) on another site — directories, industry listings, your local chamber of commerce, even old listings from a previous address. Google cross-checks these to decide how much to trust your profile. The catch: if your phone number is written one way on your website, another way on Facebook, and your old address still shows up on an outdated directory, that inconsistency quietly works against you. Before adding new citations, search your business name and clean up or claim the ones that already exist with the wrong details.

4. Reviews: volume, recency and what they actually say

Reviews feed directly into prominence, but not in the simple way most businesses assume. Google weighs how many reviews you have, how recently they came in, your average rating, and — increasingly — whether the review text itself mentions relevant keywords, like a service or a neighborhood. A business with 40 reviews trickling in steadily over two years usually outranks one with 40 reviews that all arrived in the same month and then stopped, because steady recent activity signals a business that's actually operating and cared about. Responding to every review, good or bad, is also a signal Google tracks, not just a courtesy to the customer.

Quick test: search your own business by name plus your city, then search a generic term a customer would actually type. If you don't appear in the map pack for the generic search, that's the gap — being found for your own name doesn't help anyone who doesn't already know you.

5. What your website still needs to carry

The local pack pulls some of its relevance signal from your website, so it's not a free pass to ignore it. Your city or service area should appear naturally in your homepage copy and page titles, not just buried in a footer. If you genuinely serve multiple towns, a dedicated page per area works far better than one page trying to rank everywhere at once — as long as each page has real, distinct content and isn't a copy-pasted template with the city name swapped. LocalBusiness structured data (schema markup) in your site's code, and an embedded Google Map on your contact page, both reinforce the same address and details Google already has on file.

6. Local links and mentions that build prominence

Prominence also comes from being mentioned outside of Google entirely: a write-up in a local news site, a sponsorship listed on a community event's page, a partnership page from a supplier or industry association, a listing in your city's business directory. These carry more weight than another generic directory submission because they're harder to fake and genuinely tie your business to a real place. You don't need dozens — a handful of legitimate local mentions usually outperforms a long list of low-quality directory listings that add nothing but a duplicate NAP entry.

7. The mistake that quietly caps most local businesses

The most common failure isn't a technical one — it's treating the Google Business Profile as a one-time setup task instead of something to maintain. Categories go stale as a business adds services, photos stop getting uploaded, questions on the profile go unanswered, and posts stop appearing. Google can tell the difference between a profile that's actively maintained and one that was filled in once and forgotten, and it factors that into prominence. The businesses that consistently show up in the pack are usually the ones still adding a photo or a post every few weeks, not the ones with the fanciest profile from launch day.

Checklist to improve your local pack ranking

  • Business categories on your Google Business Profile match what customers actually search for
  • Name, address and phone number identical across your website, Google, Facebook and every directory
  • Old or duplicate listings with outdated details found and corrected or removed
  • Reviews coming in steadily, not just in bursts, and every one gets a reply
  • Your city or service area mentioned naturally on your website, not just in a footer
  • At least a few genuine local mentions or links beyond directory submissions
  • Profile actively updated with new photos and posts, not left untouched since setup
